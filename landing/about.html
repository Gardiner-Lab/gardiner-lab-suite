Why it exists

A plasmid is designed in one place, its sequence kept in another, its glycerol stock written in a shared inventory, and the seeds it eventually produces listed in somebody's spreadsheet. Each of those works on its own. What fails is the join: two years on, nobody can say which construct a line carries, who made it, or what was done to it.

Trellis Tools keeps those records in one place and links them as they are made, so the join is never something anyone has to reconstruct afterwards.

The tools

MoClo Library Tool

The lab's Golden Gate parts, compatibility checking, assemblies, reaction mixes and a CRISPR guide designer.

Plasmid Editor

Sequence editing and annotation, restriction analysis, cloning simulation, primer design and sequencing alignment.

Lab Notebook

A page for every day, active tables, gel photos, a shared protocol library, and links to everything else.

Seed Bank

Lines, seed lots and plants, with the lineage that connects them, phenotypes, photos and 3D scans.

Lab Stocks

Glycerol stocks, primers, chemicals, antibodies and plant material, with labels, an order list and eLabJournal.

Booking

Rooms and equipment on a week calendar, shared with the labs that use them, shown in the notebook on the day.

Backups

Every database copied to the lab's NAS each night, with a console to check and restore them.

Each has its own fuller description and guide: open a tool and press About or Help.

What holds it together

  • One sign-in. Sign in once at the front door; every tool knows who you are.
  • One set of labs. A lab is defined once, and every tool honours it.
  • One lab code. Your lab's short code begins every code it issues, in every tool, so GL-G-0001 (a glycerol stock) and GL-S-0001 (a seed lot) belong together.
  • Links made as the work happens. Confirming a clone makes its glycerol stock; harvesting a plant makes the next seed lot; typing @ in a page links a plasmid, stock or seed, and that record then lists the page in return.

Labs, people and sharing

  • Your lab's records are the lab's: stocks, seeds, plasmids, protocols and equipment are shared with everyone in it, so they stay when people leave.
  • Your notebook is yours until you share it — a page at a time, or the whole thing with your lab.
  • Chair groups let neighbouring labs see each other's stocks and seeds read-only: enough to borrow an antibody or cross with a mutant, never enough to change someone else's records.
  • Equipment is shared deliberately, with the labs or chair groups its owner chooses.

Alongside eLabJournal

Trellis Tools does not replace the institution's record. Where eLabJournal is used, Lab Stocks copies stocks there with their box and position, brings over what eLab already holds, and takes back what changed. In a group shared with other labs, each stock keeps its real owner, so nobody's work is credited to the wrong person.

Your data

Everything is on the lab's own server, in plain SQLite databases with the photos and files inside them, backed up to the lab's NAS every night. Nothing is sent anywhere else, apart from the eLabJournal copy when that is switched on.

Nothing disappears quietly: notebook pages keep their earlier versions, records keep a history of who changed what, deleted pages can be brought back, and a deleted record is hidden rather than erased so that whatever points at it keeps working. Each release is installed after a snapshot and a count of every record, compared again afterwards.

Open source

Free software, written for the Gardiner Lab at Utrecht University and published at github.com/Gardiner-Lab. Each tool carries its own licence and its own attributions — the Lab Notebook follows Trilium's design, and the Plasmid Editor's feature set is modelled on ApE (A Plasmid Editor); see each tool's About page.

Another lab can run the whole suite with one command, and each lab gets its own space, its own code and its own records.
